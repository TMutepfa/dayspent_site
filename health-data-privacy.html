Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Last updated November 14, 2026

Some information you give DaySpent, such as how you're feeling, can count as “consumer health data” under laws like Washington's My Health My Data Act. This policy explains how DaySpent, run by Takudzwa Mutepfa, handles that data. It adds to our Privacy Policy.

Consumer health data we collect

CategoryExamplesWhy we collect it
Mood and wellbeing check-insHow you feel (overworked, bored, helpless or disconnected), how productive your day felt, your check-in notes and voice notes turned into textTo suggest a reset that fits your day, and to show your weekly summary
Self-care activityResets suggested to you, resets you saved, and your ratings of themTo suggest resets you're more likely to find helpful
Guide conversationsWhat you write to the AI guide about how you're doing, and its repliesTo run the Guide, if you allow AI features
Safety signalsWhether something you wrote suggested you might be at riskTo show crisis support, as described in our crisis protocol
Information linked to the aboveYour account ID, display name, age, gender, career and recharge styleTo personalise suggestions and run your account

We collect only what's needed to provide the features you choose to use. We don't collect precise location, and we don't use geofencing.

Where it comes from

Directly from you, when you check in, use the Guide or record a voice note. Some of it is created by the app from what you give us, such as suggested resets and safety signals. We don't buy consumer health data or collect it from anyone else.

Who receives it

We don't sell consumer health data, and we don't share it with advertisers, data brokers, employers or insurers. We disclose it only to these processors, which handle it on our behalf and under contract:

ProcessorWhat it receivesPurpose
Supabase (supabase.com/privacy)All the categories aboveSecure storage, logins and hosting
OpenAI (openai.com/policies/privacy-policy)Check-in details and Guide conversations, only if you allow AI featuresAI-written suggestions, Guide replies, voice transcription and safety checks

We have no affiliates that receive consumer health data. We'd ask for your separate, specific consent before sharing it with anyone else, unless the law requires the disclosure.

Your consent

You give consent for AI features on a separate screen after sign-up, which lists what's sent to OpenAI. You can withdraw it at any time in Profile → AI features. When you do, nothing more is sent to OpenAI.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • Know and access what consumer health data we hold about you, and who we've disclosed it to. Profile → Export my data gives you a copy straight away.
  • Delete it. Profile → Delete my account deletes it straight away, or see our account deletion page. We also tell our processors to delete it. Copies in backups are removed as those backups expire, within six months at most.
  • Withdraw consent to AI features, as described above.

To make any request, email dayspentsupport@gmail.com from the address on your account, so we can confirm it's you. We'll respond within 45 days, and may extend that once by another 45 days if necessary, in which case we'll tell you within the first 45 days and explain why. Requests are free, at least twice a year.

Appeals: if we decline your request, you can appeal by replying to our decision with “Appeal” in the subject line. We'll answer in writing within 45 days and explain our decision. If you're not satisfied, you can file a complaint with the Washington State Attorney General's Office at atg.wa.gov/file-complaint.

How we protect it

Consumer health data travels over encrypted connections and is stored in a database with rules that let only you read your check-ins, notes and Guide conversations. Only the people who run DaySpent can access it, and only when needed to run the service or keep it safe.
