Crisis protocol

Last updated November 14, 2026

If you need help now

In the United States or Canada, call or text 988 at any time. In the UK or Ireland, call Samaritans on 116 123. In Australia, call Lifeline on 13 11 14. Anywhere else, find a line at findahelpline.com. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number.

DaySpent is a self-care app for working adults. It is not a crisis service, therapy or medical care. This page explains how DaySpent responds when something someone writes suggests they may be thinking about suicide or self-harm, or may be in danger.

The AI guide is an AI

DaySpent's Guide is an AI, not a person, and it says so. It introduces itself as an AI guide, every reply is labelled “AI guide”, and a note under the chat says it isn't a therapist or a medical service. It's instructed never to claim to be human, never to diagnose or discuss treatment or medication, and to encourage people to turn to people they trust rather than depend on it.

How DaySpent spots risk

Every check-in note, every Guide message, and every message and caption sent in a Lounge room is checked for signs of risk before anything else happens. The check looks for language about suicide, self-harm, wanting to die, being unsafe at home or harming someone else. It's deliberately cautious: we would rather show support when it isn't needed than miss someone who needs it.

For people who allow AI features, check-ins and Guide messages also get a second check: an AI model is asked whether the text shows risk that the first check might have missed. If that second check can't run, the first check still applies.

Crisis lines in each country

DaySpent uses the phone's region setting, not its location, to show crisis lines for where the person is, and people can choose a different country on the support screen. We list a line only after checking it against the service's own website or an official health or government source, and we describe it as open around the clock only when that source says so. We currently list verified crisis lines for the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, Kenya and India. Everywhere else, the app shows the local emergency number where we've verified it, and links to findahelpline.com, an international directory of crisis lines. It never shows another country's numbers. We re-check the list at least every six months.

What DaySpent won't do

  • Suggest relaxation or breathing exercises to someone who may be in crisis.
  • Present the AI guide as a therapist, counsellor or human.
  • Knowingly allow content that encourages self-harm. The Guide is instructed to respond to risk only with support and crisis contacts, and shared images showing self-harm are blocked before anyone else sees them.
  • Contact emergency services on someone's behalf. DaySpent isn't monitored around the clock and can't send help.

Limits

No automated check is perfect. It can miss risk expressed in unusual ways, and it can show support to someone who doesn't need it. That's why crisis support is always available in the app, whatever the checks find.

Records

When a Guide message is flagged, the flag is saved with that message in the person's own private Guide history, so the support button stays with it. Flags on check-ins and Lounge messages aren't stored. Flags are never shared with other members, employers or anyone else. See our Privacy Policy.
